# YC technical claim control — R0

## Safe description of the current company stage

We are investigating salt-derived sodium electrochemistry and developing a manufacturing-first battery platform. Our first laboratory work package is a reproducibility study of a published aqueous sodium-birnessite reference cell. We are evaluating whether safer aqueous and inorganic solid-state architectures can improve the trade-offs between cost, energy density, charging rate and manufacturability.

## Claims supported by external literature (not by our own work)

- A 2019 Nature Communications paper synthesised sodium-rich birnessite with nominal composition Na0.27MnO2 and reported a symmetric aqueous Na2SO4 cell.
- That paper reports 83 mAh/g after 5,000 cycles at 1 A/g on its stated electrode-material mass basis. This must not be represented as a pack-level energy density.
- A 2024 aqueous Na-ion study reported 88.9 Wh/kg at 0.5C and 13,000 cycles at 10C for a different chemistry, with its own mass basis and experimental conditions.
- These are literature benchmarks, not company experimental results.

## Claims not yet supported

Do not claim that our company has:

- invented a new battery chemistry;
- reproduced the 2019 paper;
- measured any energy density, power density, charging rate, cycle life or safety result;
- achieved 5x current battery energy density;
- achieved 1,500 km of vehicle range or five-minute charging;
- proven zero toxicity, zero fire risk, no gas generation or zero thermal runaway risk;
- completed a manufacturing process, production CAD, pilot line, yield analysis or unit economics;
- qualified for automotive, aviation, defence or grid deployment.

## Suggested YC answer for current pre-prototype status

"We are at the research-validation stage. We have selected a published aqueous sodium-birnessite reference as our first reproduction benchmark and are structuring a three-track programme covering aqueous sodium, oxide solid-state sodium and a conventional sodium-ion manufacturing benchmark. We have not yet invented or validated a proprietary cell. Our first milestone is to reproduce the reference material and electrochemistry with independent characterization, then use measured data to decide which design constraints are worth pursuing commercially."

Do not imply that the literature study is company IP or company experimental data.
