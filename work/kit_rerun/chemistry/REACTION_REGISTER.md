# Chemistry and reaction register

All reactions below are classified by evidence level. A schematic host reaction is not a complete reaction mechanism when hydration, defects, side reactions or surface redox contribute.

## Track A — R0 aqueous sodium-birnessite

### Material precursor oxidation/precipitation (idealised bulk balance)

`3 MnCl2·4H2O + 6 NaOH + 1/2 O2 -> Mn3O4 + 6 NaCl + 15 H2O`

This is an elemental mass-balance equation for the nominal precursor conversion. It is not a proposed elementary reaction sequence. The published method reports aqueous precipitation from MnCl2·4H2O and NaOH, followed by isolation/washing/drying.

### Sodium insertion / birnessite formation

The paper describes Mn3O4 -> Mn5O8 -> Na-rich birnessite during thermal treatment in air with NaOH. Because the final layered material is disordered and hydrated and the exact oxygen/water balance depends on measured composition, the programme does not invent a single overall balanced equation for this solid-state transformation. Measure Na/Mn by ICP-OES/MS and structural water by TGA.

### Electrochemical host reaction (schematic)

`Na_xMnO2·yH2O ⇌ Na_(x−δ)MnO2·(y−ε)H2O + δNa+ + δe−`

The counter electrode performs the reverse insertion reaction in the symmetric cell. The paper identifies co-deintercalation/co-intercalation of water with Na ions at higher potentials. This equation is a bookkeeping representation and does not capture every proton, surface-redox, defect or gas-evolution pathway.

## Track B — NZSP oxide solid-state sodium concept

### Ceramic electrolyte identity

`Na3Zr2Si2PO12` is a common NZSP composition family member used as a sodium-ion-conducting ceramic. The composition/phase and actual conductivity must be measured on the chosen powder and sintered body; formula identity alone does not guarantee dense or conductive electrolyte.

### Candidate cathode reaction (NVP comparator)

`Na3V2(PO4)3 ⇌ Na_(3−x)V2(PO4)3 + xNa+ + xe−`

### Candidate hard-carbon reaction (schematic)

`C + xNa+ + xe− ⇌ Na_xC`

These are candidate host reactions, not a validated full cell with NZSP. Do not use sodium-metal anode in the initial safety-constrained programme. The key experiment is chemical and electrochemical compatibility at both electrode/NZSP interfaces.

## Track C — conventional sodium-ion benchmark

Track C uses a published Na3V2(PO4)3/C and hard-carbon full-cell reference system as a process and performance benchmark. Its conventional organic liquid electrolyte is not intrinsically non-flammable. It is a control arm for electrode manufacturing, formation, full-cell balancing and performance measurement—not the desired end product.

## Reactions not approved for R0

- Do not electrochemically generate sodium metal from common salt.
- Do not react metallic sodium with water or aqueous brine.
- Do not make sodium hydroxide by an improvised home chlor-alkali cell; industrial chlor-alkali systems intentionally co-produce hazardous chlorine and hydrogen gases.
- Do not use chloride salt as a drop-in replacement for the paper's sodium sulfate electrolyte.
