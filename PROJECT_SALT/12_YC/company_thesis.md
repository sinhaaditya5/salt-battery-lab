# Company thesis (honest version, 2 Nov 2026)

**Problem.** Stationary batteries in hot, theft-prone or fire-regulated places are served by lead-acid, which dies early in heat, and lithium, which needs cooling and fire engineering.

**Breakthrough hypothesis (unproven, testable).** A salt-iron molten-salt cell can be made cheap enough if:
- (i) a ≤ 0.5 mm β''-alumina separator works with ≥ 100 mAh/cm² salt-iron cathodes (gate G4); and
- (ii) the molten-salt cell line costs ≤ ~$100 per kWh/yr of capacity.

Today's model puts the cell at ~$380/kWh at 1 GWh. **Both conditions must hold; neither has any evidence yet.**

**Differentiation.** None at the chemistry level (prior art: Coetzer, Battelle, Inlyte, FZSoNick). A possible edge is local manufacture in India for hot-climate telecom, if the K4 buyer test shows a priced premium.

**Initial customer / product.** Hypothetical: a 48 V, 5–10 kWh hot-site telecom battery. No customer has been contacted.

**Technical milestones and kill gates.**
- K4 buyer test (4 weeks).
- G5 seal (week 8).
- G1 replication (week 12).
- G2/G3 loading and CE (week 16).
- G4 thin ceramic (week 20).
- Stop at the first failed gate.

**Capital.**
- ~₹12.5 lakh in parts plus partner-lab time for R0.
- A pilot line would be ~$30 M (scenario); the first commercial plant ~$150 M+ (scenario).

**IP strategy.** Freedom-to-operate review of US10615407B2 (Battelle) before any product decision. File only if G4 produces a non-obvious structure or process.

**Regulatory path.**
- IEC 62984 series (product), UL 1973 + UL 9540A (siting), UN 38.3 (UN 3292 transport).
- India: TEC GR and CEA regulations (to be read).

**Founder execution plan.** Recruit an electrochemist with molten-salt or ceramic-electrolyte experience before any lab spend. Without one, do not start.

**Investability.** **Too early and technically unproven for an investment claim.** The software alternative (see `application.md`) is the investable idea on today's evidence.
