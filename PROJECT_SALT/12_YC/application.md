**Replacement bullet** (add to "Who are your competitors?"):
> - **Molten-salt "salt batteries" (sodium-nickel/iron-chloride):** FZSoNick already sells non-flammable salt-and-nickel telecom batteries; Inlyte (US) and Altech/Fraunhofer (Germany) are commercialising iron-salt and nickel-salt versions. They run at ~200–300 °C inside, store ~100–140 Wh/kg, and have not displaced lead or lithium in telecom, which tells me non-flammability alone has not been worth a large premium so far.

## 2. Replacement for "How far along are you?" (adds the new work, same honesty)
> Pre-prototype; no cell built. I first asked whether any battery made from common salt could reach my original targets: 360 Wh/kg, a 5-minute charge for 1,500 km, and $3 cost. I built a model of 30 chemistries, calibrated against seven real cells to within 15 %. **The answer is no.**
> - No sodium chemistry reaches 360 Wh/kg even optimistically; the best is ~260–290.
> - A 1,500 km car battery charged in 5 minutes needs ~3.8 MW, about 3× the best charger deployed today.
> - Salt is nearly free, but the cell is not. Ceramic, foils, electrolyte and the factory dominate cost.
>
> The only chemistries where salt itself is the active material are the molten salt-iron/nickel cells. I designed a lab test cell for them (CAD, drawings, test electronics, and a build manual with pass/kill gates), but my factory model says they cost ~4–5× sodium-ion at scale. Next: 20 buyer interviews before any lab spend.
